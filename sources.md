"Is this building out of copyright?" is the wrong question, and answering it is how people get into
trouble. There are **three** separate questions, and they have different answers for the same
building.

<div class="big-note" markdown="1">

### The three questions

| | What it covers | Typical answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · The building** | Modelling it, measuring it, photographing it | Often free. In the US, works built before **1 Dec 1990** get no protection as architectural works |
| **2 · The drawings** | Copying, tracing or reproducing a sheet | **Usually still protected.** Drawings are *pictorial works*, protected independently of the building — the pre-1990 rule does not touch them |
| **3 · The photographs** | Reusing someone's photo | Separate again, and usually protected unless made by a government survey |

The trap is question 2. Mies died in 1969, Le Corbusier in 1965, Aalto in 1976, Wright in 1959 —
their drawings are administered estates with licensing desks, however old the buildings are.

</div>

## The one reliable answer: government survey programmes

The cleanest royalty-free drawings in the world are the ones **made by a government surveyor**
rather than by the architect. They are public domain by construction, not by expiry, so there is
nothing to compute and nobody to ask.

### HABS / HAER / HALS — United States

The [Historic American Buildings Survey](https://www.loc.gov/collections/historic-american-buildings-landscapes-and-engineering-records/about-this-collection/)
and its siblings, run by the National Park Service since 1933 and held at the Library of Congress.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Scale | **581,000+ measured drawings**, 46,000+ sites |
| Contents | Measured drawings, large-format photographs, written histories, field notes |
| Licence | Public domain. Everything transmitted to HABS is placed in the public domain **as a condition of transfer**. LoC advisory: *"no known restrictions on publication"* |
| Covers | Photographs **as well as** drawings — this is the rare case where the photos are free too |

**This is the answer for any American building**, and the coverage is far better than people expect.

| Building | Survey | Drawings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Frederick C. Robie House](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/il0039/) — Wright, 1910 | HABS IL-1039 | **25** measured drawings, 9 photos, 20 data pages |
| [Edith Farnsworth House](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/il0323/) — Mies, 1951 | HABS IL-1105 | 8 measured drawings, 32 photos, 54 data pages |

**44 Frank Lloyd Wright designs** have been digitised in HABS alone.

## Public domain by age

Where no survey exists, a drawing can still be free because it is simply old enough.

### Frank Lloyd Wright — the Wasmuth Portfolio, 1910

*Ausgeführte Bauten und Entwürfe von Frank Lloyd Wright*, Ernst Wasmuth, Berlin, 1910–11. One
hundred lithographs of Wright's own drawings — plans, elevations and perspectives of work from
1893 to 1909. Published more than 95 years ago, therefore **public domain in the United States**,
and carried with a Public Domain Mark on the
[Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/Wasmuth1911).

It covers Robie House, Unity Temple, the Larkin Building, Ward Willits and the Darwin Martin House.

<div class="warn" markdown="1">
#### Wright's *later* drawings are not free

Wright died in 1959. Everything after the Wasmuth period — Fallingwater, the Johnson Wax Building,
the Guggenheim, the Usonians — is administered by the **Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation**, whose
archive moved in 2012 to the Avery Library at Columbia and MoMA. Fees may be charged by both the
Foundation as rights holder and Avery as physical owner.

So "Frank Lloyd Wright is free" is wrong. **The 1910 publication is free.** The rest is not.
</div>

## Do not assume: the names that are still protected

| Architect | Died | Free from | Administered by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mies van der Rohe** | 1969 | ~2040 | MoMA / ARS New York / VG Bild-Kunst |
| **Le Corbusier** | 1965 | ~2036 | Fondation Le Corbusier + ADAGP |
| **Alvar Aalto** | 1976 | ~2047 | Alvar Aalto Foundation |
| **Kenzo Tange** | 2005 | 2076 | Estate |
| **Sakakura Junzo** | 1969 | ~2040 | Estate |
| **Frank Lloyd Wright** (post-1910 drawings) | 1959 | ~2030 | Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation |

Two of these deserve specific warnings:

- **Le Corbusier is the strictest.** The Fondation, his universal legatee, asserts moral *and*
  economic rights and requires prior permission for reproduction of **buildings, furniture and
  texts** — not merely drawings — with mandatory © FLC-ADAGP credit.
- **Alvar Aalto is not free, and it is priced.** The Foundation holds roughly 90,000 original
  drawings and sells images from **€30**, explicitly *"including licence and copyright fee and
  royalties"*. Aalto is a licensing transaction, not a public-domain source.

## Japan

**The term.** Japan moved from life+50 to life+70 in December 2018, and **did not apply it
retroactively**. Anything already expired stayed expired. In practice:

- Architect **died 1967 or earlier** → public domain
- Architect **died 1968 or later** → life + 70

So Tatsuno Kingo, Josiah Conder, Katayama Tōkuma, Itō Chūta and Yamada Mamoru (d. 1966) are clear.
Sakakura Junzo (1969), Antonin Raymond (1976), Maekawa Kunio (1986) and Tange (2005) are not.

**Pre-modern architecture is entirely free.** Katsura Imperial Villa, temple and shrine
architecture, machiya, sukiya — no living author, nothing to clear. Modern *measured drawings* of
them are a separate work with their own copyright, so check the survey, not the building.

**Freedom of panorama is broad.** Japan's Copyright Act Article 46 permits free use of
architectural works permanently sited in public places. Modelling a Japanese building from
observation is generally fine.

**Where to look.** The [NDL Image Bank](https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/imagebank) and
[NDL Digital Collections](https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/dlib/standards/opendataset/index.html) hold
out-of-copyright Japanese material, including historic architectural publications, with open
datasets.

## Singapore

An honest answer: **there is no clean royalty-free measured-drawing archive.**

**The National Archives** holds over **270,000 building plans**, some back to **1884** — a
remarkable collection. But the
[NAS terms](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/maps_building_plans/faq) are explicit: unauthorised
reproduction is a copyright violation, and where a plan is still protected **you must identify and
approach the copyright owner yourself**. NAS does not hold rights information beyond its catalogue.

Under the **Copyright Act 2021** the term is life + 70, so plans by architects who died in 1955 or
earlier are very likely free — but that is a determination you make *per plan*, and the architect of
a 1920s shophouse plan is often not recorded.

**What is unambiguously free** is Singapore government *data*, under the
[Singapore Open Data Licence](https://data.gov.sg/open-data-licence) — a *"worldwide, perpetual,
royalty-free, non-exclusive"* licence covering [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/),
[OneMap](https://www.onemap.gov.sg/) and SLA datasets, commercial use included, with attribution.

That is excellent for **site and context** — which is exactly what
[Stage 01]({{ '/stages/pre-design/' | relative_url }}) needs — and no use at all for building
drawings.

## If you want to change this course's building

The strongest fully-free alternative is the **Robie House**, and it is arguably better documented
than the one this course uses:

| | Robie House | Farnsworth House |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HABS measured drawings | **25** | 8 |
| Architect's own drawings | **Free** — Wasmuth 1910 | Protected until ~2040 |
| Structure | Steel-reinforced masonry, famous cantilevers | Welded steel frame |
| Teaching value | Complex plan, roof geometry, art glass | One room, pure module |

Robie is **double-sourced**: a public-domain government survey *and* the architect's own
public-domain publication. That is as clean as this ever gets, and it would let the course compare
a drawing Wright made with a drawing a surveyor made of the same building — which is a genuinely
good exercise about what a measured drawing is *for*.

<div class="warn" markdown="1">
#### And the rule that makes all of this mostly moot

**Dimensions are facts.** Reading a drawing and recording that a bay is 22'-0" is not copying the
drawing. Every model in this course is built from *dimensions with sources*, and reproduces no
sheet of any kind.

Take dimensions off a drawing. Do not trace it, do not scan it into your submission, and do not use
the redrawn plans that circulate on architecture websites — those are derivative works of exactly
the drawings you may not copy, and being freely visible is not the same as being freely usable.

Nothing on this page is legal advice. Terms differ by country, estates change their positions, and
commercial publication deserves a real check.
</div>
