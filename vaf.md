## What it is for

SIA describes the BluePrint as a dynamic document detailing the essential services architects
generally provide, so that a practice can allocate time, resources and manpower deliberately —
and so that clients can see what they are and are not buying.

The argument behind it is a resourcing argument. In a 2025 pilot study with BCA on HDB BTO and
condominium typologies, SIA applied published SkillsFuture salary data against the scope of work set
out in the VAF and concluded that **fees would need to be roughly three times current levels** for
architects to carry the responsibilities now expected of them. Whatever you make of the number, the
method is the useful part: list the components, attach the grades, count the hours.

That is exactly what this course asks you to do for one small house.

## How it is structured

Every row in the BluePrint has the same shape:

| Column | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Task** | The grouping — "Brief Preparation", "Tender Preparation", "Compliance Check" |
| **Stage** | When it happens — Pre-Design, Detail Design, Construction Gateway, Post Completion |
| **Component** | The individual service — the unit you actually resource |
| **Relevant Authority** | URA, BCA, PUB, NParks, SCDF, LTA, NEA, SLA, and others |
| **Remarks** | What the component involves in practice |
| **Category flags** | Contract Admin · Compliance & Liabilities · Biz Ops · Design |
| **Resources** | Ten staff grades, per component |

Those ten grades are worth reading aloud, because three of them are the reason this course exists:

> Director · Senior Associate · Associate · Senior Architect · Architect · Architectural Executive ·
> **BIM Manager** · **BIM Coordinator** · **BIM Modeller** · Technical Executive

BIM is not a footnote in the VAF. It is three named grades with their own lines against components
that recur at every regulatory gateway.

## Three categories

The BluePrint splits practice into three sheets, plus a small one for business operations.

### 1 · Design Expertise

Stage vocabulary: **Pre-Design → Concept–Schematic → Schematic Design → Detail Design → Tender
Preparation → Construction → Post Construction**.

| Stage | Components |
| --- | --- |
| **Pre-Design** | Brief preparation (assist the client) · consultant team recommendations · consultant service agreements and methods of engagement · special project screening, including personnel background checks and secure file storage · communication protocol — methods, frequency, points of contact |
| **Concept–Schematic** | Budget estimation, supporting the QS · timeline estimation from design to construction, **including defining what constitutes delay or prolongation for design consultants** · site investigation for feasibility · design development to concept level, up to design sign-off · design development to schematic level, up to design sign-off · coordination and de-conflicting across the client's departments and seniorities, other consultants and specialists, stakeholders, and the public |
| **Schematic Design** | Design development ensuring key feasibility areas are considered, up to sign-off · coordination · updated timeline estimation, with any prolongation flagged to the client against the originally contracted timeline |
| **Detail Design** | Design development to a level that can go into tender preparation, up to sign-off · coordination · updated cost estimation · a more detailed construction timeline |
| **Tender Preparation** | Design development to tender level, up to tender sign-off · coordination |
| **Construction** | Review of shop drawings from a design perspective against design intent · coordination · regular site review for alignment with the design |
| **Post Construction** | Verify that construction adheres to the design specifications · handover and DLP — check and verify defects |

Three further rows sit outside the stage sequence: **Specialty** (heritage conservation, PPVC, green
buildings, mega scale, healthcare, airport, large factories, hospitality, TOD), **Creativity and
Branding**, and **Value Engineering** — redesigning while holding the design intent.

And one row that is really a warning label: **Collaboration**. The consultants a project may involve
include mechanical, electrical and C&S engineers, DfS, ESD, façade, acoustic, landscape, arborist,
ABC Waters specialist, signage, FSE, traffic, lighting, QS, blast, security, real estate analyst,
conservation, interior design, industrial process engineers, IT, AV, coastal engineer, stage
consultant, kitchen specialist, medical planner, EIA — "and many more", with a note about the
liability of sub-consultants under the letter of appointment.

### 2 · Contract Administration

Stage vocabulary: **At Services Engagement → Tender → Construction: Contract Administration →
Construction: Site Inspection → Substantial Completion → Full Completion → Closure of Contract**.

| Stage | Components |
| --- | --- |
| **At Services Engagement** | Choosing the client–contractor contract — PSSCOC, SIA, REDAS, FIDIC, NEC4; traditional, D&B and so on · Early Contractor Involvement, and the man-hours it consumes · contract parcellation: phases, stages and the architect's role in each · the client–architect service agreement itself: fee model (percentage vs lump sum), design-only vs including contract administration, basic vs additional service, payment stages, exclusions, disbursements, definition of a change to contract, copyright, dispute settlement, termination, liability, phasing and number of procurement packages · client's responsibilities and levels of responsibility between architect and sub-consultants · scoping the packages and expected number of NSC/DSC/direct contractors · **technical requirements: BIM submission requirements including level of detail (4D BIM, FM BIM), and compliance with CORENET X** · understanding client expectations and profile |
| **Tender** | Contractor qualifying method — pre-qualification, invitation, or open tender · selection criteria: price first, quality first, or PQM · contingency, provisional and prime cost sums · whether an advanced information package is needed · preliminaries: scope, site constraints, phasing, employer requirements, mock-up schedule, subcontracting, **BIM requirements including BIM dimensions and digital twin**, labour considerations · general, particular and architectural specifications · **drawings and BIM model** · tenders for designated sub-contractors · allocation of the contract sum: retention, liquidated damages, contractor's P&A, performance bond |
| **Construction · Contract Administration** | Verification and certification of work · assessment of variations — scope, disputes, verification · review of project duration: EOT, programme, catch-up and micro-programme monitoring · directions and instructions · coordination of contract |
| **Construction · Site Inspection** | Review of setting out · manpower histogram · weekly and monthly reports · RFAs and RFIs · sub-contractors, NSC and DSC · mock-ups and factory visits · regular site inspection · regular progress and technical meetings, and setting their frequency |
| **Substantial Completion** | Issuance of certification · review of as-builts — **as-built drawings, OMM and material card sheets, with site walks and verification** · review of defects and outstanding works · site handover between contractor and client |
| **Full Completion** | Issuance of certification · review of as-builts · coordination of contract |
| **Closure of Contract** | Finalisation of account: final payments, variation orders, extensions of time, dispute resolution, defects completion |

### 3 · Compliance & Liabilities

This is the longest sheet, and the most Singaporean. Its stages are gateways rather than design
phases: **Pre-Design → Pre-Submission → Design Gateway → Construction Gateway → Construction Stage
(Pre-TOP) → CORENET X: Completion → Post Completion**.

| Gateway | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Pre-Design** | Regulatory research, and buying site information: topographical survey, site drawings, DIP, SIP, RLP and services plans |
| **Pre-Submission** | URA planning parameters — zoning, plot ratio, allowable GFA and height, outline permission, change of use, pre-application consultation · bonus schemes · conservation · SLA land betterment charge · public engagement · land owner's consent (HDB, JTC, SLA) · PUB drainage reserve and sewer corridor setbacks · LTA rail reserves, ingress/egress, transport impact assessment, parking, linkages and walking-and-cycling plan · MHA screening and blast protection · NParks green buffer, tree conservation, heritage roads · NEA environmental information, setbacks and special studies (EIS, ESA, NIA, PCS, QRA) · licensing bodies whose requirements shape the design (MOE, MSF, NEA, SFA, MOM/SPF) · SCDF purpose group and hydrant location · DSTA and CAAS flight paths · SLA cadastral survey and title · inter-authority and inter-consultant coordination · client communication |
| **Design Gateway** | URA Planning Permission and Written Permission · accessibility and connectivity · LUSH greenery · residential and strata requirements · LTA development control, parking and rail proximity · BCA universal design, approved document, productivity plan, household and storey shelter pre-consultation, design for maintainability · SCDF shelter compliance and waivers · PUB drainage, MPL and crest level · NEA environmental health, refuse and pollution control · NParks greenery and biodiversity · WSH DfS Guide 1 · MINDEF glazing · CAAS height · SLA land betterment charge |
| **Construction Gateway** | BCA building plan, accessibility, BScore, Green Mark, design for maintainability, universal design, household/storey shelter clearance, PPVC BIP approval · sustainability: air-tightness, energy performance, M&V instrumentation, submetering, carbon and resource efficiency · signage licensing · building name clearance · IRAS address · residential sale licence · SCDF notice of approval and fire safety report · PUB, NEA, LTA clearances · WSH DfS Guide 2 · SLA strata clearances |
| **Construction Stage (Pre-TOP)** | Regular site inspection for compliance, and the duty to highlight and require rectification of non-compliances |
| **CORENET X: Completion** | The as-built clearance column — amendment plans, record plans, as-built buildability score, as-built Green Mark score, façade and glazing declarations, approved-document declarations, accessibility, cable readiness, BDIP, dwelling-unit requirements, SCDF TFP application, LTA and PUB clearances, NEA reports, SP electrical, DSTA/CAAS as-built height, SingPost letterboxes |
| **Post Completion** | Amendment BP and **CSC** · Green Mark verification · **FSC** · NParks planting completion and reinstatement · LTA post-implementation review and vesting of road reserve · PUB survey and leak tests · NEA clearances |

Then two rows that close the sheet. **Additional Design Services — beyond compliance**: design for
maintainability, design for sustainability (Green Mark; super low and positive energy),
**Integrated Digital Delivery (IDD, including VDC and BIM)**, universal design index, Design for
Safety, and design for buildability (PPVC, PBU, precast). And, with no stage and no end date:
**Duty of Care (Lifetime)** — the architect's liability under common law.

### 4 · Business Operations

Small but pointed: software licences including project-specific hosting, lithography, employment
passes, professional liability insurance. The 2024 additions are LTA-driven — active mobility
checklist and plan, farm development lodgements, the infrastructure design criteria framework, and
EV charging provision.

## Where BIM sits in the VAF

Search the Compliance sheet for **BIM** and the same component appears at four separate gateways:

> Create a BIM Execution Plan · construct the BIM model · coordinate the model with all relevant
> parties for clash detection, design resolution and compliance checks · integrate the model for
> submission.

At Pre-Submission it appears in full. At the Design Gateway, Construction Gateway and Completion it
recurs as coordinate-and-integrate. At Post Completion it reduces to "BIM management".

Two conclusions follow, and this course is built on both.

**Model coordination is four events, not one.** Each has its own resource line, its own clash cycle,
and its own submission integration. Stages [03]({{ '/stages/schematic-design/' | relative_url }}),
[04]({{ '/stages/design-development/' | relative_url }}), [06]({{ '/stages/construction/' | relative_url }}) and [07]({{ '/stages/completion/' | relative_url }}) mirror
them.

**The BEP is a Pre-Submission deliverable.** It is written before the Design Gateway, not
retrofitted at tender. If you have never written one, [Stage 03]({{ '/stages/schematic-design/' | relative_url }}) is where
this course asks you to.

Meanwhile, on the Contract sheet, BIM appears from the very first stage — *At Services Engagement*,
where BIM submission requirements, level of detail (4D, FM) and CORENET X compliance are settled as
**technical requirements of the appointment**. Not a modelling decision. A contract decision, made
before design starts.

## Using it to resource this project

The VAF's real utility is arithmetic. Do this once, for the bungalow, and the method transfers.

{: .steps}
1. **List the components you will actually perform.** Take the tables above and strike out everything a 600 m² landed house does not need — no strata, no TIA, no rail protection, no PWCS. What remains is perhaps forty components, which is already more than most fee proposals itemise.
2. **Attach a grade to each.** Who does it: Director, Architect, Architectural Executive, BIM Modeller? Most components need more than one, at different intensities.
3. **Attach hours.** Guess. You will be wrong, and the size of the error is itself information.
4. **Total it, and compare with the fee you would have quoted.** This is the exercise SIA ran with BCA at national scale, on your own project.
5. **Repeat it at the end of Stage 08**, against what actually happened. The gap between the two is the single most useful number this course can give you.

Keep the result in `00-brief/resourcing.md`. [Stage 01]({{ '/stages/pre-design/' | relative_url }}) asks you to start it;
[Stage 08]({{ '/stages/post-completion/' | relative_url }}) asks you to close it.

Three frameworks, three sets of stage names, one project. This is why the course states all three.

| SIA Scope of Service | VAF · Design | VAF · Contract | VAF · Compliance |

| Pre-Design | Pre-Design | At Services Engagement | Pre-Design |
| Concept Design | Concept–Schematic | At Services Engagement | Pre-Submission |
| Schematic Design | Schematic Design | — | Pre-Submission · Design Gateway |
| Design Development | Detail Design | — | Design Gateway · Construction Gateway |
| Documentation | Tender Preparation | Tender | Construction Gateway |
