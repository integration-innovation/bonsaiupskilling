Two SIA publications sit behind the eight stages. They answer different questions, and the
difference is worth understanding before you use either.

| | **Scope of Service Matrix** | **Value Articulation Framework (VAF)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asks | *What is the architect responsible for?* | *What does doing it actually take?* |
| Shape | 8 stages × 4 roles, split Basic / Additional | Task → Stage → Component, with a resource line per component |
| Use here | Defines each stage's purpose and deliverable | Supplies the vocabulary, and shows where BIM work sits |
| Source | [sia.org.sg/architects-scope-of-service](https://sia.org.sg/architects-scope-of-service/) | [sia.org.sg/sia-value-articulation-framework-vaf](https://sia.org.sg/sia-value-articulation-framework-vaf/) |

<div class="warn" markdown="1">
**Neither is mandatory, and neither is your scope.** SIA is explicit that the matrix is a reference
for what the industry norm is — an architect's actual scope is whatever the consultancy agreement
says. Everything below is a teaching summary in our own words, not a reproduction. Work from the
source documents for anything that matters.
</div>

## The four roles

The matrix's most useful idea is that "the architect" is four different jobs, and a stage looks
different in each. This course keeps the distinction, because BIM deliverables serve different roles
at different stages and conflating them is how models end up beautiful and useless.

| Role | What it is accountable for | Where the model earns its keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Designer** | The design meeting the brief, and its development to the level each stage needs | Geometry, options, spatial quality, coordination of intent |
| **Qualified Person** | Regulatory compliance and submissions to the authorities | Parameters, areas, setbacks, the submission model itself |
| **Contract Administrator** | Procurement, the building contract, certification | Quantities, schedules, revision status, evidence |
| **Design Manager / Project Administrator** | Budget, programme, consultants, communication | Progress, change control, who-changed-what-and-when |

## The eight stages, summarised

Read this as *what the profession expects*, then read the stage page for *what the model has to
contain to deliver it*.

| Stage | Designer | Qualified Person | Contract Administrator | Design Manager |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **01 Pre-Design** | Help the client set the design brief; investigate the site | Research the regulatory requirements; obtain existing approvals; consult authorities where needed | Advise on procurement method | Establish budget, programme, consultant needs, communication protocols |
| **02 Concept Design** | Offer an initial design answering the brief; help refine the brief | Keep the design compliant; consult authorities on specifics; obtain owner's consent if needed | Advise on the form of building contract | Track the design process; assist with consultant appointments; project plans and responsibility matrices; preliminary cost estimate with the QS |
| **03 Schematic Design** | Develop the design to the level Development Control submissions need; prepare outline specifications | Prepare and lodge submissions for Written Permission and other DC clearances; waivers and amendments | Input on contractual matters in the outline specification; flag early procurement | Coordinate the team; review the QS's estimate against budget; monitor programme |
| **04 Design Development** | Develop the scheme to the level Building Plan submissions need | Prepare and submit for BP/DP clearances and BP approval | Check the client's requirements for pricing; pre-qualify tenderers; formulate tender evaluation criteria | Continue budget, programme and coordination |
| **05 Documentation** | Prepare tender drawings and specifications detailed enough to price under the chosen procurement route | Advise which regulatory requirements belong in the building contract | Compile tender documents; run the pricing/tender process; facilitate the award; compile contract documents | Coordinate and communicate |
| **06 Construction** | Prepare construction drawings; attend to design issues; check the contractor's submissions and the works against design intent | Ensure permits and clearances are in place; monitor statutory compliance; inspect against approved plans | Administer the contract — orders, certification, site meetings, inspection against the contract | Monitor cost against budget and progress against programme |
| **07 Completion** | Inspect the completed works against design intent | Submit architectural as-built drawings; arrange authority inspections; obtain occupancy clearances; report to IRAS | *(covered by certification under the contract)* | Manage the handover process |
| **08 Post Completion** | *(no basic design service)* | Obtain the clearances required for statutory completion | Address latent defects and outstanding works; conclude the final account and the building contract with the QS | — |

#### The one that surprises people

An **As-Built BIM model** is an *Additional Service* at Completion, not a basic one. So are
post-occupancy evaluation, building performance studies, artist's impressions, VR walk-throughs,
physical models, basic interior design, and any documentation arising from a **design change** —
where "design change" is defined to include changes from a new authority requirement or from site
conditions, not only changes the client asks for.

If you intend to hand over an as-built model, it belongs in the agreement and in the fee. This
course produces one at Stage 07 precisely so that you know what it costs to produce.
</div>

## What the VAF adds

The VAF (SIA BluePrint V.1) decomposes practice into three categories — **Design Expertise**,
**Contract Administration**, and **Compliance & Liabilities** — and lists every component with the
staff grades it consumes, from Director down through Senior Architect, Architectural Executive,
**BIM Manager**, **BIM Coordinator**, **BIM Modeller** and Technical Executive.

Three things in it shape this course directly.

**1 · BIM is a named component at every compliance gateway.** At Pre-Submission, Design Gateway,
Construction Gateway and Completion, the same component recurs: create a BEP, build the model,
coordinate it with all relevant parties for clash detection, design resolution and compliance
checks, and integrate it for submission. That is four separate coordination efforts, not one, and
each has its own resource line. Stages 03, 04, 06 and 07 here mirror them.

**2 · Its stage vocabulary is finer than the matrix's.** The design sheet runs Pre-Design →
Concept-Schematic → Schematic Design → Detail Design → Tender Preparation → Construction → Post
Construction; the contract sheet adds *At Services Engagement*, *Tender*, *Construction: Contract
Administration*, *Construction: Site Inspection*, *Substantial Completion*, *Full Completion* and
*Closure of Contract*. Where a stage page here talks about two distinct pieces of work in what the
matrix calls one stage, that is usually why.

**3 · It names the specialists.** The consultant list in the VAF runs to two dozen disciplines —
C&S, M&E, DfS, ESD, façade, acoustic, landscape, arborist, ABC Waters, signage, FSE, traffic,
lighting, QS and on. Stage 01 asks you to work out which of them this bungalow actually needs. The
honest answer is fewer than a tower and more than none, and the exercise is knowing which.

## What the model must contain, stage by stage

The framework asks *what is delivered*; this is the modelling translation. Treat it as the level of
information need for this project — it is deliberately modest, because a bungalow does not justify
a tower's LOIN.

| Stage | Geometry | Information | Classified as IFC? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Site extent, datum, north, setback envelope | Brief, controls, budget, programme, team | Site only |
| 02 | Massing options, two or more | Areas per option, option comparison, cost order of magnitude | No — plain sketch |
| 03 | Walls, slab, roof form, rooms, principal openings | Areas per space, outline specification, planning parameters | Yes — elements and spaces |
| 04 | Assemblies with material layers, coordinated openings, junctions, fittings | Types, quantities, finishes, first IDS ruleset, clash-checked | Yes — typed |
| 05 | Frozen tender baseline | Drawings, schedules, quantities, specification, revision register | Yes — issued |
| 06 | Construction revision with approved changes only | Change records, RFIs, variations, progress status | Yes — controlled |
| 07 | Verified as-built condition | Asset data, O&M references, deviations recorded | Yes — as-built |
| 08 | Unchanged except for rectified defects | Final account reconciliation, defects closed, lessons | Yes — archived |
